BREAKING: NASA awards Modulate Space ~$38M contract to develop 5G communications for the Moon

NASA has awarded Modulate Space Corporation an approximately $38 million firm-fixed-price contract to develop key elements of a lunar-surface 5G communications system with built-in Wi-Fi 6, for NASA's Glenn Research Center in Cleveland.

NASAMoonMoonBaseLunarSurface5GGlennResearchCenterBREAKING

NASA has awarded Modulate Space Corporation a contract to develop key elements of a 5G communications system for use on the lunar surface with built-in Wi-Fi 6, the agency announced today. The work is for NASA’s Glenn Research Center in Cleveland.

The 5G System and Lunar Surface Communications Evaluation and Radio Propagation Measurements contract is a firm-fixed-price award valued at approximately $38 million. NASA describes it as an accelerated effort organized into two primary work areas:

  • 5G Network-in-a-Box Development and Lab Demonstration, with a performance period running from Oct. 1, 2026, through Jan. 31, 2028.
  • Lunar 5G and Wi-Fi Integrated Surface Network and Flight Demonstration on the Lunar Surface, with a performance period running from Nov. 30, 2026, through Dec. 31, 2028.

Together, the two work areas are meant to develop a scalable, standards-based communications capability designed to support future Moon Base surface operations. Through the demonstrations, NASA will acquire technology development of the hardware, firmware, and software needed to advance the integrated lunar communications system.

NASA said the effort will give the agency insight into the system architecture and operational performance while keeping the flexibility to evolve the technology for future lunar surface communications.

Why it matters: Sustained operations on the Moon will depend on connecting crews, rovers, landers, and science instruments across the surface, not just linking back to Earth. Building on commercial, standards-based 5G and Wi-Fi 6 rather than bespoke radios could let NASA take advantage of the terrestrial telecom ecosystem. The Network-in-a-Box work starts tomorrow, and the flight demonstration period runs through the end of 2028, so the lab and lunar-surface results will show whether off-the-shelf wireless standards can work in the lunar environment.
