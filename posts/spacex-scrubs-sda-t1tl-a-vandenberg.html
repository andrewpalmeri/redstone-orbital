BREAKING: SpaceX scrubs Falcon 9 T1TL-A — 21 SDA Transport Layer satellites from Vandenberg

SpaceX scrubbed its Oct. 5 morning Falcon 9 attempt to loft 21 Space Development Agency Tranche 1 Transport Layer satellites from Vandenberg SLC-4E after an abort in the final minute of countdown. Next try is NET Tuesday, Oct. 6, at 1:17 a.m. PDT.

SpaceXSDAFalcon9PWSATransportLayerGoldenDomeMissileDefenseBREAKING

SpaceX scrubbed its Oct. 5 morning Falcon 9 attempt to loft 21 Space Development Agency (SDA) Tranche 1 Transport Layer satellites — mission T1TL-A — from Vandenberg Space Force Base’s Space Launch Complex 4 East. The abort came in the final minute of the countdown as the rocket entered automated startup. No reason was given at the time of the scrub update.

The Northrop Grumman–built satellites are the fourth Tranche 1 Transport Layer mission for SDA’s Proliferated Warfighter Space Architecture (PWSA). The agency is building a low-Earth-orbit mesh intended to deliver low-latency missile-warning data and secure communications for the warfighter, with operational capability targeted around 2027.

Next attempt: NET Tuesday, Oct. 6, at 1:17 a.m. PDT (3:17 a.m. CT).

Three prior T1TL launches have already put 63 Transport satellites on orbit toward a planned Tranche 1 constellation of 126 spacecraft. Today’s scrubbed flight would have added another 21 birds to that proliferated backbone.

Why it matters: PWSA’s Transport Layer is the data mesh that moves missile-warning and tracking products across LEO — the connective tissue of SDA’s proliferated architecture and a Golden Dome–adjacent capability. This is a dedicated Transport Layer mission, not a Starlink rideshare.
