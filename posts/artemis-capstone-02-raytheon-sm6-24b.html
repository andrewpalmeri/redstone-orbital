Rehearsing Lunar Rendezvous and Building Up Interceptor Supply

NASA's CAPSTONE 02 will attempt the first rendezvous and proximity operations in a lunar near-rectilinear halo orbit, and the Navy awards RTX's Raytheon up to $24.4 billion for SM-6 interceptors as Golden Dome leans on proven rounds.

ArtemisNASACAPSTONECAPSTONE02ArtemisAccordsMissileDefenseGoldenDomeSM6RaytheonRTXNavy

Two of this week’s stories share one theme: proving hard capabilities before they’re needed at scale. NASA is getting ready to practice rendezvous in a lunar halo orbit, and the Navy has signed a long-term supply deal for its most versatile interceptor.

Artemis

Aerospace America (Oct. 1) details NASA’s CAPSTONE 02, led by Advanced Space. Two spacecraft of about 400 kg each, built on Terran Orbital Ambassador buses, are slated to launch next year. They will attempt the first rendezvous and proximity operations in a near-rectilinear halo orbit (NRHO). That means three-body dynamics, which Apollo’s low-lunar-orbit rendezvous never had to handle. NASA has funded $45M so far and expects about $99M total for both spacecraft. Advanced Space will own and operate them and deliver navigation, communications, thermal, and radiation data. Even after halting Gateway earlier this year, NASA calls NRHO a testbed whose lessons carry over to other cislunar orbits. Next up: the Artemis Accords Principals’ Meeting on Monday, Oct. 5, at IAC in Antalya, which will cover support for emerging space nations and lunar-debris mitigation.

Sources: Aerospace America: The next step for cislunar navigation (Oct. 1); Payload: Artemis Accords to meet on lunar debris, emerging space help (Sept. 29)

Missile Defense: Raytheon SM-6

RTX’s Raytheon has won a contract worth up to $24.4 billion for Standard Missile-6 interceptors. It runs five years plus two option years (PR Newswire, 5:20 p.m. ET, Oct. 1). SM-6 is the only combat-proven weapon that can do anti-air warfare, anti-surface warfare, and ballistic missile defense, and it has been fired from both Navy ships and land launchers. The caveat: Reuters, via Military Times, notes that Congress has not yet appropriated the money for the multiyear munitions packages, so the production ramp still depends on lawmakers. For Golden Dome context, Arms Control Today reports that Guetlein’s two-stage plan starts by integrating existing capabilities against decapitation and rogue threats by 2028. Next-generation sensing and directed energy come later, toward 2035. That makes production rates for proven rounds like SM-6 worth watching.

Sources: PR Newswire / RTX: SM-6 multiyear contract (Oct. 1); Military Times / Reuters: SM-6 award amid stockpile concerns (Oct. 1); Arms Control Today: Golden Dome to defend against decapitation, rogue attacks (Oct. 2026)
