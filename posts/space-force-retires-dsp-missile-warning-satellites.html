BREAKING: Space Force retires the Defense Support Program missile-warning satellites

The Space Force has decommissioned its Defense Support Program missile-warning constellation, ending more than 50 years of space-based early warning. The last two DSP satellites flew in U.S. Space Command's Apollo Maneuvers exercise in early September before moving to a graveyard orbit above GEO.

SpaceForceMissileWarningDSPSBIRSNextGenOPIRGoldenDomeBREAKING

The Space Force has officially decommissioned the Defense Support Program (DSP), the missile-warning satellite constellation that has watched for launches from geosynchronous orbit for more than half a century, the service’s Combat Forces Command announced Oct. 1.

In a final act, the last two DSP spacecraft took part in U.S. Space Command’s Apollo Maneuvers exercise in early September, helping the command demonstrate dynamic maneuvers across multiple orbits. After the exercise, both satellites were moved to a supersynchronous graveyard orbit above GEO.

“These last two satellites were the culminating end to over a half-century of space-based early warning,” Mission Delta 4 Commander Col. Brandon Davenport said in a statement.

The first DSP satellite launched in 1970. The constellation went on to provide Cold War deterrence and detected theater missile launches during Operation Desert Storm in 1991.

What’s still watching: DSP’s successor, the Space-Based Infrared System (SBIRS), remains on orbit with six satellites in GEO and four in highly elliptical orbit. The first Next-Generation Overhead Persistent Infrared (Next-Gen OPIR) GEO satellite is expected to launch in the coming months, adding more sensitive sensors to detect dimmer threats.

Why it matters: The Space Force’s missile-warning architecture now spans GEO, medium-Earth orbit, and the Space Development Agency’s proliferated low-Earth orbit layer, which serves as the sensor backbone for Golden Dome. Retiring DSP also frees operators in the 2nd Space Warning Squadron to focus on preparing for the launch and initial testing of Next-Gen OPIR.
