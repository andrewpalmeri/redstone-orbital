NASA says Moon Base is ahead of schedule, and signs a nuclear pact to keep it running through the lunar night

At Thursday's Golden Age Summit, NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said the first Moon Base element is planned to launch in December, with near-monthly uncrewed robotic missions from early 2027, beating the 2030 target. The same day, NASA and DOE signed a space nuclear power and propulsion agreement aimed at Lunar Reactor-1 and the 2028 Space Reactor-1 Freedom flight.

Moon Base is moving up on the calendar.

At Thursday’s Science: A Golden Age Summit in Washington, NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman told White House science office director Michael Kratsios that the agency expects to beat the national space policy’s original 2030 target for the first Moon Base elements. The first element is planned to launch in December, and near-monthly uncrewed robotic missions are set to start in early 2027 (Payload).

Power for the lunar night

The same day, Isaacman and Energy Secretary Chris Wright signed a memorandum of understanding titled “Accelerating American Leadership in Space Nuclear Power and Propulsion.” It takes effect Nov. 1 and covers end-to-end NASA–DOE work on research, fuel, testing, launch integration and operations (NASA).

Two programs sit at the center of it. Lunar Reactor-1 is the fission surface power system meant to keep a future Moon Base running through the roughly two-week lunar night, when solar power isn’t an option. Space Reactor-1 Freedom is targeted to fly in 2028 as a nuclear propulsion demonstrator, taking the technology from the lab into deep space (NASA; Space & Defense). A December 2025 executive order already calls for a launch-ready lunar surface reactor by 2030. Isaacman called it the start of the “Nuclear NASA era.”

Why it matters

Payload notes the agreement largely formalizes plans already in motion and carries no new cost figures. Even so, the signal is clear: surface power and an early robotic buildup are now the pacing items for Artemis. Short, sun-limited visits get you flags and footprints. A reactor that runs through the night is what turns a landing site into a base.

What to watch: the December launch of the first Moon Base element, how NASA sequences the 2027 robotic flights, and whether Congress funds Lunar Reactor-1 on the 2030 timeline.
