Daily Brief Oct 19, 2026

NASA sets a Friday Artemis update as an Army interceptor warning and a Russian satellite's pursuit of ICEYE raise the stakes

NASA leadership briefs Oct. 23 on Artemis III and the 2028 landing goal, after Administrator Isaacman said Kennedy has cut its stacking schedule in half. JAXA's MMX launches today with NASA instruments and a Blue Origin-built sampler, and Blue Origin announced a $330 million New Glenn expansion. On missile defense, CBS reports the Army chief warned before the Iran war that THAAD and Patriot stocks would stay strategically low until 2030. Russia's Cosmos 2612 has also closed on ICEYE-X36, a U.S.-registered radar satellite tied to Ukraine.

Artemis

Friday is the week’s Artemis marker. Administrator Jared Isaacman, Associate Administrator Amit Kshatriya, and Artemis program manager Jeremy Parsons brief at Kennedy at 1 p.m. CT Oct. 23 on Artemis III, next year’s crewed low-Earth-orbit test, and the 2028 landing goal. SpacePolicyOnline notes Isaacman said last month Artemis III was about 90 days behind. After a Friday visit, he posted Sunday that Kennedy’s teams have cut the stacking schedule by 50% and that Blue Origin’s pad-rebuild crew “has a fire in them like we have never seen before.” Over the weekend, Wiseman, Glover, and Hansen accepted the Stephen Hawking Medal for the Artemis II crew at Starmus.

Sources: NASA: Artemis acceleration briefing advisory (Oct. 14); SpacePolicyOnline: week ahead (Oct. 18); @NASAAdmin on X (Oct. 18); Space.com: Hawking Medal (Oct. 18)

NASA

JAXA’s MMX launches at 2:41 p.m. CT today on an H3 from Tanegashima with NASA’s MEGANE spectrometer and a sampler from Honeybee Robotics, a Blue Origin company; it reaches Mars in August 2027 and returns a Phobos sample in 2031. Wednesday, the National Academies release their NASA-requested report on human exploration across the Moon, including non-polar sites. Blue Origin also announced a $330 million Cape Canaveral expansion: two more New Glenn pads and capacity for up to 150 upper stages a year, aiming for multiple flights a week by 2030.

Sources: SpacePolicyOnline: week ahead (Oct. 18); CNBC: Blue Origin’s $330M expansion (Oct. 16)

Golden Dome / Missile Defense

Golden Dome’s operational arm, NORTHCOM’s Joint Task Force-Gold, has about 100 people at Peterson SFB under Lt. Gen. John Rafferty, surveying sites (Fort Story, Va., is the first confirmed hub) and standing up defensive cyber. The harder question is the magazine. CBS News reported that Army Chief of Staff Gen. Randy George warned in December, before the Iran war, that THAAD and Patriot stocks would stay strategically low until 2030. CSIS estimates the war used up to 61% of prewar Patriots and about 82% of THAAD interceptors. The Pentagon calls shortage claims false.

Sources: Air & Space Forces: JTF-Gold (Oct. 16); CBS News: George’s interceptor warning (Oct. 15)

Also on the radar: a Russian satellite stalks ICEYE

Russia’s Cosmos 2612 spent two weeks maneuvering into the orbital plane of ICEYE-X36, a U.S.-registered commercial radar satellite tied to Ukraine’s imagery work. LeoLabs told Axios the approach looks deliberate. Tracker Marco Langbroek’s data point to a pass early Oct. 18 within a few hundred meters or closer, with no sign ICEYE-X36 was disturbed. DoD and ICEYE declined comment.

Sources: CSIS: Cosmos 2612 analysis (Oct. 15); Axios: Russia nears U.S. satellite (Oct. 17); SatTrackCam Leiden: Langbroek tracking (Oct. 17-19); Aviation Week (paywalled) (Oct. 19)
