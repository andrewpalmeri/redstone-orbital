Daily Brief Oct 30, 2026

NASA eyes June for Artemis III as the Army picks Lockheed's PAC-3 Edge for its Future Interceptor

Aviation Week reports NASA is aiming to launch the third SLS in June 2027, and Blue Origin expects to finish welding the Blue Moon Mk2 test article's hull by January. Starship Flight 15 moved to Nov. 12 on FAA planning data. The Army awarded Lockheed Martin a $1.08 billion Future Interceptor agreement for PAC-3 Edge, while Golden Dome waits on the Dec. 11 funding deadline.

Artemis

Artemis III now has a month attached. Aviation Week reports NASA is aiming to launch the third Space Launch System rocket in June, firming up the summer 2027 target from last week’s briefing; the full story is paywalled, so only the public caption is used here. The lander side is moving too. NASASpaceFlight reports Blue Origin is fabricating the dome and barrel panels for its Blue Moon Mk2 test article in Florida, with final hull welding expected by January 2027, before avionics work and encapsulation in New Glenn’s seven-meter fairing. Administrator Jared Isaacman told Florida Today the schedule has no give: “So that mission must fly in ‘27,” he said, because data from the integrated lander tests feeds Artemis IV.

Sources: Aviation Week: NASA Firms Up Plans for Artemis III Lunar Lander Tests (paywalled; public caption only) (Oct. 30); NASASpaceFlight: Blue Origin attempts end of year return and eyes Artemis IV selection (Oct. 29); Florida Today: NASA chief Jared Isaacman says Artemis III ‘must fly’ in 2027 (Oct. 29)

NASA, SpaceX and Blue Origin

SpaceX has now static-fired both Flight 15 stages, finishing a six-engine Ship 42 test on Oct. 27, and plans to try catching both Ship and Super Heavy if regulators approve. FAA airspace planning data dated Oct. 29 moved the flight to Nov. 12 from Nov. 8; that is a planning marker, not a launch date.

Blue Origin has all nine Vehicle Access Tower modules at LC-36A repaired and restacked and is laying the foundation for a second pad, LC-36B. Closer to home, it launched ATLAS, a registered machinist apprenticeship in Huntsville with Drake State, starting with a 10-person cohort. NASA also named Troy Asher director of Armstrong Flight Research Center.

Golden Dome / Missile Defense

The Army picked Lockheed Martin’s PAC-3 Edge for its Future Interceptor, announced from Huntsville Thursday. The Other Transaction Agreement is worth an estimated $1.08 billion: $660.4 million from the government and $423 million in Lockheed cost share. It funds prototypes and testing against operational requirements, with production decisions to follow. Lockheed says it plans to start testing in 2027 and reach residual operational capability by 2031, reaching higher and farther than PAC-3 MSE while working alongside it and THAAD.

Golden Dome itself had no new milestone. JTF-Gold’s next planning sprint was set for the end of October, and the stopgap that blocks new Golden Dome starts runs out Dec. 11.
