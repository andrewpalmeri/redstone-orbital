Leidos protests Space Force’s $572M award to run NORAD’s Cheyenne Mountain missile-warning system

Leidos has challenged Amentum’s seven-year, $572 million NISSC III win at Cheyenne Mountain, arguing Space Force mishandled price and unequal treatment. GAO is due to rule by Jan. 29 — after the incumbent contract expires Dec. 25.

Leidos has protested the Space Force’s award of a seven-year, $572 million contract to Amentum to operate and maintain the missile-warning system inside NORAD’s Cheyenne Mountain Complex. The Government Accountability Office is expected to rule by Jan. 29, 2027.

The award, announced in late September, is the third iteration of the NORAD Cheyenne Mountain Complex Integrated Tactical Warning/Attack Assessment and Space Support Contract — NISSC III. Work covers system administration, vulnerability management, organizational-level maintenance, and test support for the NCMC-ITW/AA system that correlates sensor data on adversary launches and attacks, then warns the National Command Authority, the National Military Command Center, combatant commanders, and Canada’s national leadership.

Amentum is the incumbent. Jacobs won NISSC II in 2020 under the OASIS vehicle; Amentum took that federal-services business in a 2024 merger. About $547 million in orders flowed through the prior contract. Space Force recompeted the work as an Alliant 2 task order and received seven offers. The new ceiling is $572,358,987, with about $15.6 million in fiscal 2026 operations-and-maintenance money committed at award. Performance runs through Sept. 27, 2033, centered at Peterson Space Force Base, Colorado, through Space Systems Command’s Battle Management Command, Control, Communications and Space Intelligence office.

What Leidos is arguing

Per Washington Technology’s reporting on the protest filing, Leidos contends that:

  • Price was not properly considered in the best-value decision.
  • The government made unsupported assumptions about proposal revisions.
  • Discussions with bidders were unequal, and bidders were treated differently.
  • Amentum’s incumbent past performance was evaluated improperly.

Neither Leidos, Amentum, nor the Space Force has issued a detailed public statement on the merits beyond the award announcement and trade-press coverage of the protest claims.

The timing problem

NISSC II expires Dec. 25, 2026 — more than a month before the GAO’s Jan. 29 decision deadline. A June bridge notice had already flagged that a 45-day government shutdown delayed the planned June award and that the government intended a short bridge with monthly options to cover transition and any protest. Whether a formal stay of the new award is in force has not been confirmed in public reporting; continuity of the warning system through year-end will almost certainly depend on that bridge or an extension.

Why it matters for the missile-defense beat

This is sustainment of a legacy space command-and-control and attack-assessment stack, not a Golden Dome modernization buy. Still, the system is the one that tells U.S. and Canadian leaders whether a launch is real. As Golden Dome raises the political and budget stakes around missile warning and battle management, who runs — and how the government awards — the computers under Cheyenne Mountain is not a side story. Possible GAO outcomes are the usual three: deny the protest, sustain it and order corrective action, or see the agency take corrective action on its own before a ruling.
