Daily Brief Nov 2, 2026

NASA won't commit SLS for Artemis III until Blue Moon is in orbit, as Google eyes a Golden Dome data role

Administrator Jared Isaacman told NASASpaceFlight that Artemis III will not commit the crewed SLS launch until Blue Origin's Blue Moon test lander is in orbit and checked out, naming the lander as the schedule driver. NASA opened a commercial-servicing RFI for Hubble, Swift reenters this week, and Starship Flight 15 still awaits its FAA license. Bloomberg reports Google is in early talks on a Golden Dome data-security group, and State cleared a $2.1 billion, 500-interceptor PAC-3 MSE sale to Ukraine paid for by allies.

Artemis

The crewed launch for Artemis III now waits on Blue Origin. In a NASASpaceFlight interview published Saturday, Administrator Jared Isaacman said NASA will not commit the Space Launch System until Blue Moon is already in orbit. “We’re not going to launch without Blue Origin in it,” he said. New Glenn flies the Blue Moon test lander first, and it can loiter about 30 days for checkouts. SLS and Orion follow with the crew, then Starship. Isaacman named the lander as the main schedule driver: “We don’t have a fully assembled Blue test article yet.” He added that NASA has flown C-17s to move lander domes cross-country and sent hundreds of staff to help with pad recovery and welding. He said NASA would accept “slipping another month” if it gets more out of the Blue mission.

Sources: NASASpaceFlight: Isaacman outlines Artemis III multi-launch checkout sequence and Blue Origin priority (Oct. 31); NASA: NASA’s Artemis III Mission to Test Future Moon Lander Operations (Oct. 23)

NASA, SpaceX and Blue Origin

NASA issued a request for information Oct. 29 on commercial servicing for Hubble: a reboost, upgrades, or a controlled reentry. Responses are due Nov. 30, and NASA says any mission would need to fly by 2032. Swift, which a robotic rescue failed to save, is predicted to reenter around Nov. 4, give or take about a day.

Starship Flight 15 has both stages static-fired but no FAA license yet for its return-to-launch-site profile and first try at catching the Ship. Blue Origin’s official line remains a New Glenn return to flight before year-end; Aviation Week has reported a November target, which the company has not confirmed with a date.

Golden Dome / Missile Defense

Bloomberg reports Google is in early talks to join a Pentagon industry group that would protect the data feeding Golden Dome’s command-and-control software, with Aalyria involved. A Pentagon official said no decision has been made on the group or its members. That would be Google’s first publicly known Golden Dome work.

On missile defense, State approved a possible $2.1 billion sale of 500 PAC-3 MSE interceptors to Ukraine, paid for by Canada, Germany, Norway and EU-backed loans, with no U.S. funds. MDA also posted a Low-Cost Kill Vehicle solicitation under its NOBLE announcement. The Dec. 11 funding deadline still hangs over new Golden Dome starts.
