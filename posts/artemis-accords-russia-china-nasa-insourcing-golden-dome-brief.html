Daily Brief Oct 7, 2026

Artemis Accords leave the door open to Russia and China as NASA pulls 7,500 contractor jobs in-house

At IAC Antalya, NASA Deputy Administrator Matt Anderson said Russia and China could one day join the Artemis Accords if they share its principles, ahead of today's first Artemis II lunar science data release. At home, NASA's push to convert about 7,500 contractor positions into civil-service jobs is now reaching Artemis ground crews at Kennedy, and senators are pressing for answers on Golden Dome's cost and coverage.

Artemis

Artemis diplomacy took the stage in Antalya. At Wednesday’s Artemis Accords briefing, NASA Deputy Administrator Matt Anderson said Russia and China could join only if they became “like-minded in those principles, which we don’t see them as that right now.” Just over 40 of the 76 signatories attended Tuesday’s closed-door principals’ meeting, which reached agreement on space debris; Anderson’s line: “The first step to stop space debris… is to stop creating it.” He kept the Accords civil-only, leaving weapons issues to Space Force and Space Command, and said member nations want to contribute to NASA’s Moon base. NASA releases its first comprehensive Artemis II lunar science data set at IAC this afternoon (session starts 8:35 a.m. CT). On hardware, Axiom expects NASA’s call soon on a simplified suit for early landings, and Artemis III’s twin SLS boosters finished stacking Sept. 30 for a June 2027 target.

Sources: Anadolu — Anderson on Russia/China (Oct. 7); IAF — Artemis II science session (Oct. 7); SpaceNews — Axiom suit (Foust) (Oct. 7); Florida Press — Artemis III boosters stacked (Oct. 1)

NASA

Isaacman’s workforce initiative is converting about 7,500 contract positions into civil-service jobs to rebuild “core competencies.” Ars Technica reports NASA had about 40,000 contractors and 14,000 civil servants before the push, and now has roughly 21,000 civil servants. Isaacman cites more than $100 million in early savings, possibly over $1 billion in time; Amentum, Jacobs and KBR warn of lost flexibility during the Moon push. NASA’s Aerospace Safety Advisory Panel called it “a coherent and necessary vision.” The impact is now visible on Artemis ground teams: Amentum filed a WARN notice for 70 Kennedy layoffs effective Nov. 30, including 11 test conductors, as NASA insources work under its $3.2 billion COMET contract. Separately, Crew-12 undocked at 7:05 a.m. CT today; splashdown off California is set for about 10:34 a.m. CT Thursday.

Sources: Ars Technica — 7,500 conversions (Berger) (Oct. 6); Florida Today — Amentum WARN notice (Oct. 5); NASA — Crew-12 undocks (Oct. 7)

Golden Dome / Missile Defense

The money problem is now a credibility problem. Axios reports senators remain “in the dark” on Golden Dome’s architecture; Sen. Angus King said nationwide coverage appeared “not in the cards,” asking “who is protected and who isn’t?” CBO said it got its first Golden Dome office briefing just five days before the Sept. 29 hearing. Almost all of the $17.5 billion FY27 request rides on a stalled reconciliation bill, and the program has no CR baseline. On the hardware side, the Army picked 30 finalists, including RTX, Anduril, Northrop Grumman and L3Harris, for its $8 million xTech Apex Intercept prize for low-cost Patriot-compatible interceptors, with up to 18 winners by Dec. 31. SDA’s T1TL-A (21 Northrop Grumman transport satellites) is now NET Thursday, 2:54 a.m. CT, from Vandenberg.

Sources: Axios — senators question feasibility (Oct. 7); The Hill — funding uncertainty (Oct. 2); Overt Defense — xTech Apex Intercept finalists (Oct. 6); SpaceX — T1TL-A (Oct. 7)
