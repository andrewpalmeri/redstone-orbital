From Space Command rivals to Golden Dome teammates: Huntsville and Colorado Springs join forces as Artemis III watches its landers

More than 90 Colorado Springs leaders are in Huntsville this week, and SPACECOM's Gen. Stephen Whiting used Monday's joint event to back a new "twin-city partnership" on missile warning, tracking, command and control, and interceptors as the HQ move to Redstone Arsenal keeps building. Meanwhile, an Artemis III astronaut says the 2027 mission is on track "but anything can happen," and Golden Dome starts FY27 with no usable funding.

Huntsville + Colorado Springs

Former Space Command rivals are now pitching themselves as Golden Dome teammates. The Colorado Springs Chamber & EDC brought a 90-plus delegation to Huntsville Oct. 4-7, including Mayor Yemi Mobolade, El Paso County commissioners and city council members. Mobolade and Huntsville Mayor Tommy Battle held a joint news conference Monday, and executives from Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman, Boeing and L3Harris were in the room; more than 70 defense firms operate in both cities. No formal agreement was announced. At the evening reception, Whiting called the pair “not just any old ‘sister cities,’ but true strategic cities,” adding, “Unexpected allies are the best allies.” In a statement, he welcomed “the novel formation of this twin-city partnership.” Mobolade: “competition and collaboration are not mutually exclusive.” On Golden Dome money, the Huntsville/Madison County Chamber’s Chip Cherry said the mission hasn’t gone away, “so it’s just the timing of the funding.” The move continues: more than 150 of SPACECOM’s roughly 1,800 personnel are at Redstone, with about 200 expected by year-end, half by end-2028, groundbreaking in 2027 and full move-in by 2032. Parsons won an $85M owner’s-advisor agreement Sept. 29 for the 950,000-square-foot campus. Colorado’s lawsuit against the move is still pending.

Artemis

Artemis III still targets 2027, but the landers set the pace. Mission specialist Andre Douglas told Reuters the low-Earth-orbit docking rehearsal could fly next summer, though “anything can happen” with SpaceX’s Starship and Blue Origin’s Blue Moon still in development; Blue Origin is still recovering from the May 28 New Glenn loss, traced to a BE-4 oxygen valve. Suits are next: Aviation Week reports NASA asked Axiom Space to study a stripped-down “sortie suit” for Artemis IV and V. At IAC Antalya, Deputy Administrator Matt Anderson told Anadolu the civilian race with China is “closer than we would like right now,” and that NASA has “exciting news to announce very soon.” The Artemis Accords principals (76 nations) met Monday on lunar debris and emerging-space support; an Accords news conference and a preliminary Artemis II lunar science release follow Wednesday. Crew-12 is set to undock from the ISS no earlier than 8:05 a.m. EDT (7:05 a.m. CT) Wednesday, Oct. 7.

Golden Dome / Missile Defense

Golden Dome begins FY27 effectively unfunded. The Pentagon put $17B of its request in a reconciliation package Congress shows little appetite to pass, with only $400M in the base budget. Because the program had no FY26 appropriation, the continuing resolution that took effect Oct. 1 and runs through Dec. 11 gives it nothing to build on. About 90% of the initial $25B is already obligated. CSIS’s Tom Karako put it bluntly: “the program has no money.” Some senators are pushing a bipartisan fix. On the space layer, SpaceX stood down Tuesday’s try for SDA’s T1TL-A (21 Northrop Grumman transport satellites) after Monday’s auto-abort; it’s now NET Thursday, Oct. 8, 12:54 a.m. PT (2:54 a.m. CT).
