NASA sets Oct. 23 Artemis Acceleration briefing as Golden Dome stands up cyber defense

NASA will brief on Oct. 23 on accelerating Artemis: Artemis III is now a 2027 low-Earth-orbit test of commercial lander systems with SpaceX and Blue Origin, and the first crewed lunar landing is targeted for Artemis IV in 2028. A SpaceX cargo Dragon on CRS-35 is due to dock at the station this morning with the final roll-out solar arrays. At AUSA, NORTHCOM's Joint Task Force-Gold said defensive cyber for Golden Dome is a new mission set, with an initial capability targeted by year-end.

Artemis

NASA is putting dates on the revised Moon architecture. On Oct. 14 the agency announced a media update for 1 p.m. CT Friday, Oct. 23 at Kennedy Space Center — billed as Artemis Acceleration — with Administrator Jared Isaacman, Associate Administrator Amit Kshatriya, and Artemis program manager Jeremy Parsons. The advisory says the briefing will cover Artemis III sending four astronauts to low Earth orbit next year to test critical systems for future landings, and progress toward returning Americans to the lunar surface by 2028.

That framing matches what Isaacman told USA TODAY in an interview published Oct. 14: under his watch NASA made Artemis III a LEO test mission and shifted the first crewed surface return to Artemis IV in 2028. A WIRED interview with Artemis III scientist Barbara Cohen, published this morning, fills in the mission design — Orion in Earth orbit rendezvousing with SpaceX and Blue Origin lander test articles (not full lunar landers) before those same maneuvers are attempted near the Moon. Cohen also described dress-rehearsal sample-handling work using cabin witness materials. Hardware progress continues in parallel: Artemis III boosters are stacked and all four RS-25s are on the core for the short-stack tanking campaign.

Sources: NASA: Artemis Acceleration briefing advisory (Oct. 14); USA TODAY: Isaacman on Artemis restructure (Oct. 14); WIRED: Cohen on Artemis III LEO test (Oct. 15); NASA: Artemis III booster stacking (Oct. 13)

NASA

CRS-35’s cargo Dragon is in the final hours of its chase. Coverage starts about 8:15 a.m. CT with docking targeted around 10 a.m. CT at Harmony’s zenith port, carrying more than 6,300 pounds of cargo including the station’s final pair of roll-out solar arrays for Crew-13. Crew-12 meets the press this afternoon after nearly eight months on orbit. Separately, Space Force activated Space Forces Special Operations (SPACEFOR-SOC) to support U.S. Special Operations Command — a formal ceremony is set for Nov. 2 at MacDill.

Sources: Space.com: CRS-35 docking today (Oct. 15); NASA: CRS-35 launch (Oct. 13); SpaceNews: SPACEFOR-SOC activation (Oct. 13–14)

Golden Dome / Missile Defense

Golden Dome’s operational arm is adding a cyber mission. At AUSA on Oct. 14, Col. Kevin “Stoney” Stonerook, deputy commander of NORTHCOM’s Joint Task Force-Gold (~100 personnel at Peterson SFB, Colo., double-hatted under Army SMDC’s Lt. Gen. John Rafferty), said defensive cyber warfare for Golden Dome for America “has become a new mission set.” Defense Daily reports an initial stage of cyber protection and passive anomaly detection is planned by year-end. JTF-Gold is also in the siting work for the first operational site at Fort Story, Va., and will run another planning sprint at month’s end on roles among the three major ops centers at Peterson, Schriever, and Tyndall. Gen. Michael Guetlein’s publicly reported Stage 1 (by 2028) still centers on coercive decapitation and rogue-nation attack; Stage 2 (by 2035) brings next-gen tech. FY27 Golden Dome money beyond a thin base-budget line remains tied to unresolved reconciliation and the continuing resolution.

Sources: Breaking Defense: JTF-Gold cyber for Golden Dome (Oct. 14); Defense Daily: initial cyber by year-end (Oct. 14)
