Daily Brief Nov 3, 2026

NASA's Artemis III team details SLS and Blue Moon plans as Swift nears reentry and Leidos protests a NORAD contract

At a Huntsville symposium, NASA and industry leads said SLS will fly Artemis III without its upper stage, with a pad tanking test targeted by year-end, while Blue Origin expects to restart its New Glenn pad in December. Swift is due to reenter Nov. 4, SpaceX's USSF-432 Falcon 9 launch now shows Nov. 6, and Leidos has protested the $572 million Cheyenne Mountain support award to Amentum. Golden Dome had no new milestone, and its funding still hinges on a stalled reconciliation bill.

Artemis

Artemis III’s hardware plan came into sharper focus at the American Astronautical Society’s von Braun symposium in Huntsville (panel held Oct. 26, write-up published Nov. 2). NASA’s SLS program said the rocket will fly without its Interim Cryogenic Propulsion Stage, with a spacer in its place. The goal is to roll SLS to the pad without Orion by year-end for a full tanking test of the core stage’s redesigned hydrogen seals. Orion’s docking module goes on in the coming weeks.

Blue Origin expects to activate Launch Complex 36 in early to mid-December and still aims to fly New Glenn by year-end. Welding of the Blue Moon Mark II test article’s crew module begins early next year. SpaceX will launch its Starship test article from Pad 39A. NASA is targeting mid-2027, and SpaceNews says the flight sets up landing attempts on Artemis IV and V in 2028.

Sources: American Astronautical Society: Artemis III: Practicing in Earth Orbit for the Return to the Moon (Nov. 2); SpaceNews: The 2028 Imperative: NASA’s 2028 crunch for Artemis, SR-1 and robotic lunar landings (metered; opening paragraph only) (Nov. 3)

NASA, SpaceX and Blue Origin

NASA’s Swift observatory is nearly out of orbit. As of Monday, the Space Force predicted reentry around 3:30 a.m. EST Wednesday, Nov. 4, give or take 11 hours, mostly over ocean. NASA puts the risk of harm at about 1 in 2,100 and expects some pieces to survive. NASA has named no successor.

SpaceX’s mission page now targets Friday, Nov. 6 for the Falcon 9 USSF-432 launch from Vandenberg. Starship Flight 15 has finished both static fires but still awaits its FAA license. The FAA is also reviewing Blue Origin’s request to raise New Glenn cadence at SLC-36 from 12 launches a year to as many as 50; comments closed Oct. 30, and approval is not guaranteed. ULA still lists Vulcan’s return to flight for Nov. 13 with 40 Amazon Leo satellites.

Golden Dome / Missile Defense

Golden Dome had no new milestone. Google’s reported data-security talks remain early, per Bloomberg on Oct. 30. Money is still the constraint: the stopgap runs to Dec. 11, and roughly 95% of the fiscal 2027 request depends on a reconciliation bill Congress has not passed. Today is Election Day, and the Senate is not expected to act on a budget resolution before it.

Leidos has protested Space Force’s $572 million NISSC III award to Amentum, a seven-year contract that includes maintenance of the Cheyenne Mountain system that tracks adversary launches. Leidos says price was not properly considered and bidders were not treated equally. GAO is due to rule by Jan. 29. Separately, a $95 million Lockheed GPS IIIF modification, announced by DoD Oct. 30, settles costs from a production gap. It adds no satellites.
