BREAKING: NASA says Artemis III is on track for summer 2027

At today's Artemis Acceleration briefing, NASA said Artemis III will launch four astronauts in summer 2027 on a 14-day mission in low Earth orbit, docking with both Blue Origin's Blue Moon and SpaceX's Starship test landers. Artemis IV still targets a crewed south pole landing in 2028.

BREAKINGArtemisNASABlueOriginSpaceXHLS

At today’s “Artemis Acceleration” briefing at Kennedy Space Center, NASA said Artemis III is on track to launch four astronauts in summer 2027 on a 14-day mission in low Earth orbit. The crew will fly on SLS and Orion and dock with both commercial lunar landers. Blue Origin launches a Blue Moon test lander first, and Orion docks with it. SpaceX then launches a Starship test lander, and Orion undocks from Blue Moon and docks with Starship.

That puts three heavy-lift rockets (SLS, New Glenn, and Starship) on one mission. It’s a full rehearsal of the rendezvous and docking a landing needs before Artemis IV, which still targets a crewed landing at the lunar south pole in 2028. NASA had left Artemis III’s duration and lander plan open (“one or both”); committing to both landers keeps both companies in the running for Artemis IV. Administrator Jared Isaacman, Associate Administrator Amit Kshatriya, and Artemis program manager Jeremy Parsons led the briefing.

Note: NASA’s own post-briefing release and a full transcript weren’t available at publish time. Details come from Florida Today’s public summary (paywalled) and the syndicated copy. We’ll update if anything materially changes.
