Daily Brief Oct 26, 2026

NASA lays out the Artemis III flight plan and reroutes both Moon landers; Golden Dome's funding clock runs toward Dec. 11

At Friday's briefing NASA held Artemis III to summer 2027 and published a 14-day Earth-orbit plan to dock Orion with Blue Origin's and SpaceX's landers, dropped the halo-orbit rendezvous for both Artemis IV-era landers, and said it is working with ULA on an extended Centaur V to put Orion in low lunar orbit. Golden Dome still has no fiscal 2027 money, and the Space Force moved its first Next-Gen OPIR missile-warning satellite from Vulcan to Falcon Heavy.

Artemis / NASA

NASA used Friday’s Kennedy briefing to hold the line: Artemis III is still targeted for summer 2027, and the first crewed landing, Artemis IV, for 2028. Administrator Jared Isaacman called it “an achievable path.”

The Artemis III plan is now public. Blue Origin’s Blue Moon test article launches first on New Glenn. Then SLS, flying without an upper stage, puts Randy Bresnik, Frank Rubio, Andre Douglas and ESA’s Luca Parmitano into orbit in Orion. Orion docks with Blue Moon on day three, and two astronauts spend two days inside the lander. A Starship test article launches on day six; Orion docks with its nose for about two hours, with no crew transfer. The baseline is 14 days.

The bigger change is how crews reach the Moon. Both landers drop the near-rectilinear halo orbit. A fully fueled Starship lander will dock with Orion in Earth orbit and push the stack to low lunar orbit. Blue Origin defers propellant transfer and uses three transfer stages, four New Glenn launches in all, to carry Blue Moon Mark 2 to an elliptical polar lunar orbit. NASA also said it is working with ULA on an extended Centaur V upper stage so Orion itself can reach low lunar orbit, a fix Isaacman said was “left unaddressed for far too long.” NASA will pick the first lander based on readiness. On Starship refueling, Isaacman called Musk’s six-to-eight-tanker figure possible but the “ultimate potential end state,” and NASA gave no count.

Golden Dome / Missile Defense

Golden Dome had no new milestone over the weekend, and money is the story. Fiscal 2027 began without Golden Dome funding: the Pentagon requested about $17 billion through a reconciliation bill that hasn’t moved and only about $400 million in the base budget. A Defense Briefing review of contract notices from Oct. 1 to 19 found no new Golden Dome obligations. Work continues on 2025 reconciliation money, and the stopgap runs to Dec. 11, after the midterms. JTF-Gold’s next planning session is due at the end of October.

On sensors, the Space Force moved the first Next-Gen OPIR GEO missile-warning satellite and an experimental cislunar surveillance payload from Vulcan to Falcon Heavy, part of the six-mission swap driven by Vulcan delays.
